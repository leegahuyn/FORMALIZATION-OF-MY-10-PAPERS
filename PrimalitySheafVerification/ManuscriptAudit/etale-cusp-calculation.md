# Étale cohomology of two singular curve fibers

Papers 2, 4, 5, and 6 identify a dimension difference of ordinary étale cohomology with a compact-support Euler characteristic difference. Paper 2 pp. 40–41 also puts a contribution from the local delta invariant into the quotient of constant sheaves along normalization. [Arithmetic cusp cohomology](ArithmeticCuspBump.lean) gives dimensions 1 and 2 for the cusp and puncture over F₅ with constant ℤ/2 coefficients. [Geometric cusp cohomology](GeometricCuspBump.lean) gives dimensions 0 and 1 over the algebraic closure with prime-to-characteristic coefficients. Lean verifies both signed differences as −1. The compact-support calculation uses the open–closed localization cited below.

Let $K=\overline{\mathbb F_5}$, let $\ell\ne 5$ be prime, and set $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z$. Let $X=\operatorname{Spec} K[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)$, let $o$ be its origin, and set $U=X\setminus\{o\}$. The normalization is $\nu\colon\mathbb A^1_K\to X$, with $x=t^2$ and $y=t^3$. It is finite since $t$ is integral over $K[t^2,t^3]$. Away from the origin its inverse is $t=y/x$. At the origin there is a single point above it with the same residue field. These facts make it a finite, surjective, universally injective morphism, hence a universal homeomorphism. The smooth locus is $U\cong\mathbb G_{m,K}$. The same equation defines a flat finite-type family over $\mathbb Z$ because the coordinate algebra is free of rank two over $\mathbb Z[x]$ with basis $1,y$. Its geometric fiber at 5 is $X$.

- Topological invariance along $\nu$ identifies ordinary étale $H^1(X,\Lambda)$ with $H^1(\mathbb A^1_K,\Lambda)$. The latter is zero by the Kummer sequence, since $K[t]^\times=K^\times$ and $\operatorname{Pic}(K[t])=0$. On $U$, the Picard group is also trivial, and $K[t,t^{-1}]^\times=K^\times t^\mathbb Z$ gives $H^1(U,\Lambda)\cong\Lambda$. The [affine Picard and polynomial unit file](CuspPicard.lean) concerns the actual coordinate rings. The [punctured cusp file](CuspPunctured.lean) constructs the localization comparison. In both applications $\mu_\ell\cong\Lambda$ after choosing a primitive root in $K$. [Étale topological invariance](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03SI), [Kummer sequence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03PK)
- The signed ordinary degree-one difference is $0-1=-1$. Applying compact-support cohomology to the open–closed localization sequence gives $\chi_c(X)-\chi_c(U)=\chi_c(\{o\})=1$. [Open–closed sequence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03S2), [Compactly supported cohomology](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0GJY)

The normalization induces an equivalence on the étale site, so $\Lambda_X\to\nu_*\Lambda_{\mathbb A^1}$ is an isomorphism. The quotient of these sheaves is zero. ## A projective node with zero ordinary bump

Paper 5, pp. 153–154, and Paper 6, pp. 197–199, include detector comparisons for curve fibers. The ordinary degree-one difference can vanish on a singular fiber. The following calculation is a mathematical counterexample. Its cohomological steps are pending in Lean.

Over $K=\overline{\mathbb F_5}$ consider the projective cubic $Y^2Z=X^2(X+Z)$ and write $o=[0:0:1]$. On the affine chart $Z=1$, the equation is $y^2=x^2(x+1)$, irreducible because $x+1$ is not a square in $K(x)$. The derivatives vanish together on this curve only at $(0,0)$. Its two distinct tangents are $y=\pm x$. At $[0:1:0]$ the derivative in $Z$ is nonzero. This example is a fiber of a flat projective model over $\mathbb Z$. The defining polynomial is primitive, so the homogeneous quotient is torsion-free over $\mathbb Z$ by Gauss's lemma. The degree-zero parts of the standard localizations remain torsion-free and are flat over $\mathbb Z$.

The map $[t:u]\mapsto[u(t^2-u^2):t(t^2-u^2):u^3]$ gives the normalization $\nu:\mathbb P^1_K\to X$. Off $o$ on the affine chart it has inverse parameter $t/u=y/x$, and the point at infinity has a single preimage. The preimages of $o$ are $[1:1]$ and $[-1:1]$. The map is proper with finite fibers, so it is finite, and the affine inverse makes it birational. The smooth open $V=X\setminus\{o\}$ identifies with $\mathbb P^1_K\setminus\{[1:1],[-1:1]\}\cong\mathbb G_{m,K}$. [Coordinate checks](NodalNormalizationCoordinates.lean), [Finiteness criterion](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02LS)

For prime $\ell\ne5$ and $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z$, the map $\Lambda_X\to\nu_*\Lambda$ is an isomorphism off $o$ and the diagonal $\Lambda\to\Lambda^2$ at $o$. The quotient is the skyscraper $i_*\Lambda$ at $o$. The two connected curves have the same constant global sections and $H^1(\mathbb P^1_K,\Lambda)=0$ by Kummer and $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1_K)=\mathbb Z$. Finite pushforward and the resulting long exact sequence give $H^1(X,\Lambda)\cong\Lambda$. Also $H^1(V,\Lambda)\cong\Lambda$ by the multiplicative group calculation above. Thus the ordinary degree-one difference is $1-1=0$ at this singular fiber, while $\chi_c(X)-\chi_c(V)=\chi_c(\{o\})=1$. [Finite pushforward](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03QN), [Projective Picard group](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BXJ), [Kummer sequence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03PK), [Open–closed sequence](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03S2)

The [finite-field point counts](CuspPointCounts.lean) give exactly q points on the affine cusp and q−1 on its puncture over each finite field of order q. The normalization parameter identifies these point sets with the field and its nonzero elements. This calculation holds over all finite extensions.
